## Talk:

### Take home message:
Asteroids are not just a bunch of rocks in space, but they diverse, have impact on your life, and can encode information about the history of our solar system. 

### Audience persona:
Dee-Dee is in high school and is interested in science and space. He loves almost every sci-fi movie that he has seen, and has seen Jurassic Park more times than he can count. Ever since he went to the space museum once on a school trip and saw the space shuttle, he has been fascinated by the idea of space exploration. Though he is curious about space, he is not doing very well in his science classes and is slowly being disillusioned. Both his parents are in blue collar jobs. They have been supportive of his interests, and have made an effor to nurture his curiosity as he grew up, but they are also slowly encouraging him to focus on more practical career paths based on his grades and their own experiences. 

### Content ladder:
- What are asteroids? What are they made of?
- How, when and where are they formed in the solar system?
- They are in different regions currently than where they formed originally. 
- They are influenced by the gravitational pull of the big planets, and can be nudged into different orbits.
- This change can also lead them to collide with other planets, including Earth.
- Since their orbits are influenced by the gravitational pull of the planets, they can be used as data points to understand the history of our solar system. 
- How do we study them? Space missions to visit them, and telescopes to observe them, including amature astronomers.
